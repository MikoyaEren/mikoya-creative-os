## Format and look

- 9:16 vertical, 720 x 1280.
- Bright, polished 3D cartoon animation with simplified anatomy and expressive characters.
- High-contrast cinematic lighting: warm skin and home scenes, saturated purple antagonists, gold/green solution energy.
- Bold uppercase word-synced captions near the lower center, white with a dark outline. Treat captions as an editing layer, not part of image-generation prompts.

## Pacing

- The scene detector reached 80 distinct cuts by approximately 2:22, indicating an average visual beat around 1-2 seconds in the mechanism-heavy section.
- Voiceover sections last roughly 2-7 seconds, but a section may contain multiple related visual beats.
- Change the visual whenever a new mechanism, symptom, ingredient, time milestone, or sales point is introduced.
- Let a generated clip show one action. Represent rapid editorial cuts as separate storyboard clips rather than asking a video generator for internal cuts.

## Hook pattern

- Open immediately in first person: the problem introduces itself as an expressive antagonist.
- Put the antagonist directly on or inside the affected organ so the premise reads without explanation.
- Literalize each phrase quickly: a calendar for “years,” a sleeping woman for hidden damage, then a move inside the liver for the first mechanism.
- Use direct-to-camera gestures and facial expression to create a confrontational, mischievous tone.

## Narrative sequence

1. Personify the hidden problem.
2. Explain internal mechanisms with literal anatomical metaphors.
3. Show relatable symptom scenes with a recurring protagonist.
4. Introduce failed alternatives as characters that explain their limitations.
5. Introduce ingredients one by one as distinct helpers with simple powers.
6. Reveal the product as the container and delivery mechanism.
7. Show absorption and mechanism-of-action visuals.
8. Present a day/week transformation timeline using the same protagonist.
9. Contrast old frustrations with the improved state.
10. Close with guarantee, bundle offer, scarcity, product close-up, and a phone-based CTA.

## Visual devices

- Anthropomorphize diseases, hormones, nutrients, capsules, droplets, and the product bottle.
- Use one obvious metaphor per frame: blocking a conveyor, breaking a barrier, energy switching on, a shield protecting absorption, or a villain being defeated.
- Alternate macro anatomy with familiar lifestyle scenes to prevent visual fatigue.
- Reuse the protagonist through the transformation section so improvement reads emotionally even when individual frames change.
- Give the product repeated clean hero shots during the mechanism explanation and CTA.

## Canonical character and object locks

Use these locks when the current reference is the supplied Hashimoto's calibration ad. Repeat the relevant lock verbatim in every text-to-image prompt; do not replace it with “same character” or a short label.

### Hashimoto's antagonist

“A round plum-purple cartoon creature with a smooth spherical body, stubby blue arms and legs, large white eyes, heavy dark eyebrows, and a wide mischievous toothy grin.”

Keep the spherical silhouette, purple body, blue limbs, eye shape, eyebrows, and toothy grin consistent. Expressions may move from smug to angry, but the face and proportions must not change.

### Recurring woman

“A young adult woman with warm medium skin, a short wavy black bob, large brown eyes, a soft oval face, and an average build.”

Use a fitted black short-sleeve T-shirt and black pants as the default wardrobe. For bedroom scenes, substitute pale patterned pajamas; for shower scenes, substitute a white robe or shower-safe framing. Always repeat the complete physical descriptor before the wardrobe change.

### Husband

“An adult man with warm medium skin, short neatly styled brown hair, brown eyes, a friendly angular face, and an average build, wearing a navy short-sleeve polo.”

### Aktora bottle

Use a supplied product-reference image whenever possible. Keep the amber glass, cap shape, green label placement, bottle proportions, and label color fixed even when exact text will be corrected during editing.

## Voice and sound locks

Use these as labeled defaults when direct audio analysis or user-provided voice references are unavailable. If the reference audio establishes different voices, replace these once and keep the replacements verbatim throughout the storyboard.

### Hashimoto's antagonist voice

“Mid-pitched, lightly raspy gender-neutral character voice with a neutral American accent, brisk conversational pace, sly timing, and a smug, taunting delivery.”

When the antagonist is visible, make it speak directly toward camera with clear mouth shapes, eyebrow performance, and emphatic hand gestures. During symptom cutaways, keep the woman silent while this same antagonist voice continues off-screen.

### Medicine and ingredient voice family

“Clear upbeat adult character voice with a neutral American accent, medium-fast explanatory pace, crisp diction, and confident helpful energy.”

Use this family for one-off medicine and ingredient introductions unless the reference establishes distinct voices. When selenium returns, repeat the exact same voice wording and delivery.

### Aktora voice

“Warm clear adult female-presenting voice with a neutral American accent, medium conversational pace, reassuring authority, and confident direct-response delivery.”

Use this voice from the Aktora introduction through the transformation timeline, offer, scarcity, and CTA. When the bottle is visible, it lip-syncs and gestures. During customer scenes, the woman and husband remain silent while the Aktora voice continues off-screen.

### Woman and husband

Keep the recurring woman and husband silent unless the script gives them dialogue. Their facial expressions and gestures react to the off-screen speaker; they do not lip-sync narration.

### Global sound palette

- Low organic rumble and soft tissue pulse for Hashimoto's and damaged-body scenes.
- Dark purple energy crackle for blocked conversion and reverse-T3 moments.
- Bright golden shimmer and soft ascending chime for ingredient actions and absorption.
- Warm, light motivational music bed under the transformation timeline.
- Clean product sparkle, subtle impact, and restrained urgency pulse through guarantee, bundle, scarcity, and CTA.
- Keep dialogue or voiceover clearly dominant; duck music and effects beneath every spoken line.
